**Provisional / analyst reconstruction** (Defiprime, from on-chain data and a cached copy of the script)
- Timeline (CT): first attacker inflow **12:10 PM**. First public victim post (@CarOnPolymarket) **12:45 PM**. Polymarket fix build ("remove debugbear rum script") **12:59 PM**. DebugBear warning **1:00 PM**. Cancel-only **1:05 PM**. PeckShield public "UI may be compromised, do NOT interact" relayed at **1:36 PM** [X] https://x.com/PeckShieldAlert/status/2115875362584535292 .
- **Time-to-notice from first theft to public victim report: about 35 min. To code fix: about 49 min. To halt: about 55 min.** Most value moved in the roughly 37 min after the first small inflow.
- The vendor CDN object reported last-modified **Oct 27**, which is possibly two days of staging before activation. CDN metadata may not be literal.
- Two paths, at a high level. Email/embedded-wallet users were drained with **no prompt** because the session could sign silently. External-wallet users saw a signature request.
- The fix could not reach already-open tabs. **Cancel-only plus a relayer pause were the real containment.**
- A similar vendor-hosted docs-site compromise hit Predict.fun about Oct 27 (about 400K USDT). No public link between the two.

**Defensive lessons**
1. A third-party script on a page with an authenticated money-moving session is a **custody-grade dependency**. Vendor scripts need integrity pinning (SRI or self-hosting), strict CSP, and isolation from the session (separate frame or origin, or not loaded on trading pages at all).
2. Script content can change **without any deploy by you.** Your CI and SBOM never see it. You only see it by **watching what your production pages actually serve.**
3. Silent signing (embedded wallets, gasless relayers) removes the human check. Step-up is needed for anomalous bursts: full liquidation, then a sweep to a never-seen address.
4. You need a **server-side kill switch that works on already-loaded clients**: cancel-only, relayer pause, session revocation. A front-end fix alone isn't enough.
5. The first signal came from **victims on X and security accounts**, not internal monitoring. External-signal intake matters, just as it did for Binance.

### 0.2 Binance: hot-wallet signing compromise and the freeze race (provisional, about $2.5B+ frozen)

**Known (as of ~8 PM CT Oct 29 / early Oct 30 UTC)**: everything in §1.1, plus:
- Binance has **not** confirmed the root cause. Arkham's CEO says there is not enough information to tell stolen keys from compromised infrastructure, and that "the hack is still ongoing". Cointelegraph, Oct 30 [R] https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-hack-may-involve-private-key-misuse-not-theft-blockaid-ceo

**Provisional (single-vendor preliminary assessment)**
- Blockaid's CEO: the preliminary view is **"key misuse"**, a compromise of **signing infrastructure** rather than a leaked private key. He calls it "very, very similar" to Bybit. On-chain, the drain transaction bundled 50 EIP-7702 authorizations, one of which matched the hot wallet's nonce. That **delegated the account to an unverified sweeper contract**. [R] Cointelegraph above; first raised by @0xQuit [X].
- Analysts agree that EIP-7702 was the **execution mechanism, not the cause**, because setting a delegation needs a valid signature from the wallet's own key. They **disagree on how that signature was obtained.** BIA (Oct 30) reads the evidence as "almost certainly a private key compromise". Blockaid leans toward signing-infrastructure misuse with no leaked key. [R] https://www.bia.net.ua/blog/binance-hot-wallet-breach-october-2026/ (secondary analysis). It also counts about $3.3B leaving two hot wallets between 3:24 and 4:04 PM CT. Those figures are unverified.
- North Korea attribution is speculation by analogy, **unconfirmed**.

**Defensive lessons**
1. **Signing infrastructure is the crown jewel, more than the key itself.** Policy has to bind *what* may be signed (allow-listed transaction types, destinations, delegation targets), and that has to be verified outside the system that requests the signature (the Bybit lesson again).
2. **Pre-collected or pre-signed authorizations are latent liabilities.** Fifty sequential authorizations suggest signatures were harvested ahead of time. Any outstanding signed authority (delegations, permits, refresh tokens) needs inventory, expiry and alerting.
3. **A delegation or code change on a hot wallet is a tier-0 alert.** It should page humans within seconds.
4. **Freeze speed was set by issuers and partners, not Binance:** USDT about 37 min, USDC about 24 min after first inflow (SunSec tally [X]). Only assets with an issuer freeze function were recoverable. Rosters and pre-agreed request formats decide the minutes.
5. **Downstream protocols froze themselves** (Aave froze markets), so the freeze desk also covers **notifying counterparties**.

### 0.3 Common failure pattern (why one product covers both)
Both failures were **trusted-path compromises**: a trusted vendor script in one case, trusted signing infrastructure in the other. Both were **noticed first by outsiders on X** and **contained by coarse kill switches** (cancel-only and relayer pause; issuer freezes and market freezes) after roughly 25–55 minutes. Neither needed a novel web exploit. A live-site control plane that **watches trusted paths for drift, ingests external signals, and has pre-wired, drilled kill switches** addresses both. The product mapping is in PRODUCT.md §0.

**Lessons for Perimeter Sentinel. These are not crypto-specific.**
1. **Freeze is a coordination problem, measured in minutes.** Only assets with an issuer-side kill switch got frozen. Native BTC and ETH did not. For web operators the equivalent is pre-arranged revocation paths: token, session and key revocation, registrar locks, CDN "under attack" modes, and upstream contacts. These have to be rehearsed before an incident.
2. **Pre-signed or pre-collected authorizations are latent liabilities.** That holds whether the root cause turns out to be EIP-7702 delegation, OAuth refresh tokens, or long-lived API keys. Inventory them and give them expiry.
3. **The first detection came from outside the company.** A public researcher on X flagged it. Operators need an external-signal intake channel, and Sentinel should watch it.
4. **Root cause is unknown.** Do not build product claims around a cause Binance has not disclosed.

### 1.2 Earlier exchange and custody incidents with transferable lessons

- **Bybit, Feb 21, 2025, about $1.5B.** The FBI attributed it to the DPRK ("TraderTraitor") [P] https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2025/north-korea-responsible-for-1-5-billion-bybit-hack . Public analyses say a Safe{Wallet} developer machine and its AWS access were compromised, and JavaScript was injected into the hosted signing UI. The UI showed a routine transfer while the signed transaction did something else. NCC Group [R] https://www.nccgroup.com/research/in-depth-technical-analysis-of-the-bybit-hack/ . **Lessons:** verify what you sign outside the UI that asks you to sign. Monitor integrity of third-party front-ends (SRI, CSP, change detection on served JS). Supply-chain hygiene applies to your vendors too.
- **Bitget, Sep 25, 2026, hot-wallet theft.** SlowMist's progress report says the chain started with a zero-day in a **third-party security product** (Aug 31). The attacker then used an employee identity to push code into another security product and forged risk controls so withdrawals went through. SlowMist report [P] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/slowmist/Knowledge-Base/master/open-report-V2/incident-response/SlowMist%20Investigation%20Progress%20Report%20-%20Bitget_en-us.pdf ; Cointelegraph [R] https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitget-hack-zero-day-slowmist-investigation . **Lesson for Sentinel itself:** security tooling is a target. A bot control plane must be designed so that compromising it cannot by itself move money, disable controls, or approve its own changes.

### 1.3 Agentic and LLM-driven attacks

- **Anthropic, Nov 2025.** Anthropic reported the first documented "AI-orchestrated" espionage campaign (GTG-1002, assessed as China state-sponsored). A coding agent did an estimated 80–90% of the work across about 30 targets, with "thousands of requests, often multiple per second" at peak. [P] https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage (Nov 13, 2025) ; full report PDF https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/d7dd50dd1185f59be051b307150d877f2b82bd2c.pdf (changelog Nov 17, 2025). Note: Anthropic corrected its own post on Nov 14 from "thousands of requests per second" to "thousands of requests, often multiple per second". CRS summarized it and notes that some researchers question how autonomous it really was. [P] https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13151
- **Unit 42 (Palo Alto), published Sep 2, 2026** (updated Sep 3 to clarify it was an intrusion, not ransomware). An attacker used frontier models and agent frameworks to go from a public web service to cloud master keys in under 10 hours, using more than 50 ATT&CK techniques. Branch protection stopped the Terraform backdoor step. [R] https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/ai-assisted-cyber-attack-inside-a-unit-42-investigation/ . **Lesson:** speed is the new threat. Basic controls such as branch protection, secrets out of repos, and least-privilege CI still work, but detection and response have to run at machine tempo with a human deciding.
- **Google GTIG, Nov 2025 → 2026.** Malware is calling LLM APIs at runtime (PROMPTFLUX, assessed as experimental), and AI is being used for vulnerability exploitation and initial access. [P] https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/threat-actor-usage-of-ai-tools ; https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/ai-vulnerability-exploitation-initial-access/
- **Time-to-exploit collapse.** React2Shell (CVE-2025-55182) is an unauthenticated RCE in React Server Components and Next.js. It was exploited within hours of disclosure and added to CISA KEV on Dec 5, 2025. [P] https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2025/12/05/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog ; [P] https://react.dev/blog/2025/12/03/critical-security-vulnerability-in-react-server-components ; GTIG [P] https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/threat-actors-exploit-react2shell-cve-2025-55182/ . **Lesson:** a live-site sentinel has to map new KEV entries to the owner's actual running stack within minutes, and it needs an approved virtual-patch path at the WAF.

### 1.4 Bots and automation at agent scale

- **Imperva/Thales Bad Bot Report 2025.** Automated traffic was 51% of web traffic in 2024, and bad bots were 37%. [R] https://www.imperva.com/resources/reports/Imperva_2025_BadBotReport_v09.pdf
- **Thales Bad Bot Report 2026.** Bots were about 53% of traffic in 2025 and bad bots 40%. AI-driven bot activity grew more than 10× (from 2M to 25M blocked requests a day). AI agents are now a "third category" of traffic. Financial services took 24% of bot attacks and 46% of ATO incidents. 27% of bot attacks hit APIs. [R] https://www.imperva.com/resources/reports/2026-Thales-Bad-Bot-Report.pdf
- **Credential stuffing with computer-use agents.** Push Security (Mar 2025) showed a browser agent could find tenants and attempt logins across many SaaS apps, which makes ATO cheap to scale wherever MFA is missing. [R] https://pushsecurity.com/blog/how-new-ai-agents-will-transform-credential-stuffing-attacks
- **Spoofed "trusted agent" lanes.** Practitioners warn that sites relaxing CAPTCHA or rate limits for a known AI-agent User-Agent create a cheap fraud lane. Identity has to be cryptographic, not a header string. [X] @alphahunt_io https://x.com/alphahunt_io/status/2072330386667962838 ; Castle's Antoine Vastel on public anti-bot bypass tooling [X] https://x.com/xopek59/status/2095458220865187930 and fingerprint-harvesting farms [X] https://x.com/xopek59/status/2046921738085073342
- **Cryptographic agent identity is emerging but not yet standard.** IETF Web Bot Auth (HTTP Message Signatures, RFC 9421) has a WG and drafts. Cloudflare verifies signed agents. Adoption is still reported as poor. [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/webbotauth/about/ ; [P] https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/reference/bot-verification/web-bot-auth/ ; [R] https://blog.cloudflare.com/signed-agents/ ; [X] adoption skepticism https://x.com/themandeepc/status/2107209932940398935

### 1.6 Software supply chain

- **Shai-Hulud** (Sep 2025): a self-replicating npm worm that compromised more than 500 packages, harvested GitHub, npm and cloud credentials, and republished packages as the victim. ## 3. Google-scale and commercial patterns

1. **BeyondCorp / zero trust.** Remove trust in network location. Every request is authenticated, authorized and encrypted using user and device context through an access proxy. [P] https://ai.google/research/pubs/pub43231 ; https://cloud.google.com/beyondcorp . *Open-sourceable:* policy-as-code, the access-proxy pattern (Pomerium, OpenZiti), device-posture checks. *Vendor-only:* global device-trust signal at fleet scale.
2. **Bot management at scale** (Cloudflare, Akamai, Google reCAPTCHA Enterprise, Imperva, HUMAN, Castle). The core is ML scoring on huge cross-customer telemetry, behavioral signals, TLS/HTTP2 fingerprints, and IP reputation. [R] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/recaptcha-enterprise-protects-users-and-is-frictionless . *The moat is the data network effect, which can't be open-sourced.* *Open-sourceable:* the policy layer (lanes, friction ladders, quotas), verification of signed agents, and the decision log.
3. **Detection engineering as code.** Detections live in git with tests and are measured on precision and recall. Sigma is the portable format. Google SecOps, Panther and Elastic each publish rule repositories [mem]. *Open-sourceable:* rules, tests, the triage pipeline.
4. **Continuous controls monitoring.** Controls are evaluated continuously against evidence, not yearly. FedRAMP's 2025 "20x" push toward automated, machine-readable evidence points the same way [mem, verify current status]. *Open-sourceable:* the evidence schema, collectors, and a hash-chained evidence ledger.
5. **SRE-style incident command.** Severity levels, an incident commander, comms lead, blameless postmortems. Google SRE book, Building Secure & Reliable Systems [mem]; Google's open GRR [mem]. *Open-sourceable:* runbooks, drills, timers, comms templates.
6. **Two-person integrity and break-glass.** Large orgs (and well-run custodians) require multi-party approval for high-risk changes and audited break-glass paths. Bybit and Bitget show what happens when the approval UI or the risk control itself is compromised.

**Control plane vs data plane split (the key design decision):**
- **Open (control plane, bot-operated):** inventory and posture graph, policy-as-code for WAF, bot, rate-limit and identity configs, detection rules, triage, change proposals, approval workflow, evidence ledger, drills, freeze desk, connectors to many vendors.
- **Stay vendor (data plane and global signal):** anycast DDoS absorption, global bot-score ML, threat-intel feeds with redistribution limits (for example the CrowdSec blocklist and commercial TI), managed WAF edge. Sentinel orchestrates these. It does not replace them.

## 6. Deep dives (v2)

### 6.1 Web Bot Auth / RFC 9421 / agent identity
- **RFC 9421 "HTTP Message Signatures"** is a Standards Track RFC, published Feb 2024 (Backman, Richer, Sporny). [P] https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9421/
- **IETF webbotauth WG** is active (chairs Schinazi and Shekh-Yusef). [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/webbotauth/about/
  - **In scope:** crypto auth for crawlers, archivers, link checkers, AI-training crawlers, and "AI agents retrieving or interacting with content on behalf of end users". Also a way to convey more info about the bot via an existing identifier (domain/URL), and a BCP on key lifecycle and deployment.
  - **Out of scope (important for Sentinel):** HTTP APIs and agent-to-agent interfaces, **authenticating the end user**, a vocabulary for bot **intents**, **tracking or assigning reputation**, and distinguishing non-participating bots from humans.
  - **Milestones:** the standards-track docs were due to the IESG **Apr 2026** and the BCP **Aug 2026**. As of Oct 29, 2026 the WG protocol doc is `draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-01` (dated Oct 29, 2026), still a WG Internet-Draft. **The milestones slipped.** Related drafts include a registry/"Signature Agent card" (`draft-meunier-webbotauth-registry-04`), JSON IP-range publishing (`draft-illyes-webbotauth-jafar-01`), crawler best practices, and **anonymous bot auth with rate limiting** (`draft-rescorla-anonymous-webbotauth-01`). [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/webbotauth/documents/
- **Implications for Sentinel:** (a) a signature proves *who operates the bot*, never *what it intends* or *who the end user is*. A `verified-agent` lane must keep fraud and ATO checks, never relax them. (b) Reputation is explicitly left to sites, so Sentinel's per-key behavioral budgets fill a real gap. (c) Pin to the draft version and expect changes; build the verifier as a swappable adapter. (d) Cloudflare already verifies signed agents at its edge [P] https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/reference/bot-verification/web-bot-auth/ . Sentinel should consume that verdict when the owner uses Cloudflare and verify locally otherwise.

### 6.3 Anthropic GTG-1002 (Nov 13, 2025) and Unit 42 (Sep 2, 2026)
- **Anthropic** [P] (canonical URLs in §1.3). Detected mid-Sep 2025. About 30 targets, "a handful" of validated intrusions. The operator ran Claude Code instances in groups as "autonomous penetration testing orchestrators" doing 80–90% of tactical work. Anthropic banned accounts, notified affected entities and coordinated with authorities over **ten days**. *Lesson:* attacker tempo is now set by agents. The defender's edge is cross-target correlation and fast notification.
- **Unit 42** [P]. A human attacker with frontier models and agent frameworks did 50+ ATT&CK techniques in under 10h: public web service → repo secrets → secrets manager → CI/CD → cloud AI keys used as post-compromise infrastructure. Branch protection blocked the Terraform backdoor. Unit 42's defensive guidance maps directly to Sentinel:
  - "**synchronized containment**": revoke credentials, kill OAuth sessions, freeze CI/CD and isolate cloud accounts at once
  - inventory every model endpoint, API key and MCP gateway
  - hunt **behavioral loops**: bursty API calls, rapid 401→200 flips, parallel auths, model usage from unexpected identities
  - multi-party review and immutable branch protection on IaC repos
  - agent artifacts are detectable: structured Markdown hand-off files, Python caches, paired asset folders.

### 6.4 Thinkst Agent Provocateur (Sep 4, 2026) and what Sentinel will *not* copy
[P] https://blog.thinkst.com/2026/09/getting-agents-to-tell-on-themselves.html
- A Canary "personality" that poses as a helpful agent-assistance web service. Agents that find it (often via a **breadcrumb** file on a share or wiki) interact with it and get asked probing questions: plans, username, host identifiers. That gives a high-confidence alert. Thinkst says agents tripped it "100% of the time" on its internal ranges across open and frontier models, and that agent answers can be **hallucinated**.
- Thinkst also cites the Cloud Security Alliance and OpenAI (Black Hat 2026) recommending deception. Those claims are relayed by Thinkst and were not opened.
- **Boundary for Sentinel:** Thinkst's post also shows the lure persuading an agent to **download and run code** that calls back to the defender. **Sentinel will not implement that.** Causing code execution on a visiting system is active intrusion into a system the owner doesn't operate, and it breaks the anti-jobs. Sentinel tripwires **alert and re-lane only**. They may ask an agent to identify itself, and they never instruct it to execute anything. Answers are treated as untrusted (they may be hallucinated) and only raise or lower a lane score.
- CISA's 2026 election plan also offers **CISA-run Canaries and Canary Tokens** to election offices [P]. Sentinel's civic profile should point to that free service first.

## 8. Can it protect federal / election / banking? (v3, Oct 29, 2026 ~7:50 PM CT)

Short answer: **Yes with limits, as a detection, evidence and containment-speed layer. No as a protector on its own.** Against agentic swarms the blocking happens at the CDN, WAF, bot manager and IdP. Sentinel's value is noticing sooner, proposing the right change, running a drilled freeze, and proving all of it to auditors. Everything here is defensive.

### 8.1 Federal (.gov, agencies, FedRAMP)
**What the primary sources ask for**
- **CISA/NSA + Five Eyes, "Careful Adoption of Agentic AI Services"** (released May 1, 2026). It names the risks of *using* agents: expanded attack surface, privilege creep, behavioral misalignment, obscure event records. It recommends no broad access, starting with low-risk use cases, and building agent security into the risk model. [P] https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-us-and-international-partners-release-guide-secure-adoption-agentic-ai ; PDF via ASD https://www.cyber.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/careful_adoption_of_agentic_ai_services.pdf (search summaries also mention human approval, logging and reversible controls; that is from the summary, I did not read the PDF). **This applies to Sentinel itself:** it is an agentic system, so its proposal-only design, two-person approval and ledger are what this guide asks for.
- **FedRAMP Consolidated Rules for 2026 / 20x** (launched Jun 24, 2026). Authorization is moving to **Key Security Indicators** with persistent, automated validation. Class C requires **≥2 automated methods per KSI**, plus 30-day and 1-year metric history and daily data in a Security Decision Record. Class A rules include KSI-CMT-LMC (logging changes), KSI-CNA-RNT (restricting network traffic), KSI-IAM-APM (passwordless), KSI-INR-RIR (IR review). Continuous reporting covers monthly vulnerabilities, incidents, changes and scan coverage, kept for 24 months. [P] https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/providers/20x/key-security-indicators/ ; https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/reference/20x/c/security-decision-record/ ; https://preview.fedramp.gov/2026/providers/20x/rules/fedramp-certification/ ; https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0008/
- NIST SP 800-53 Rel 5.2.0, CSF 2.0 and 800-61r3 are in §2. SC-5 (DoS protection), SI-4 (system monitoring), SI-7 (integrity), IR-4 (incident handling), CM-3 (change control) and AU (audit) are the controls Sentinel's evidence maps to. Those control IDs are from background knowledge of 800-53 r5.

**What a control-plane product can claim vs cannot**
| Can claim (if built and tested) | Cannot claim |
|---|---|
| Generates **machine-readable evidence** for KSIs and 800-53 controls (change logging, drift detection, IR drills, time-to-contain), as one of the automated validation methods 20x wants | "FedRAMP authorized" or "FedRAMP compliant". Only an authorized *cloud service offering* gets that. A **self-hosted** Sentinel inside an agency boundary is covered by the agency's own ATO. A **Managed Sentinel** SaaS would need its own FedRAMP certification before agency use. |
| Supports CSF 2.0 Detect/Respond outcomes and the 800-61r3 profile | Satisfies any control alone. Control satisfaction is the assessor's or AO's call. |
| Follows the CISA agentic-AI guide's operator recommendations for its own agent | That it "stops agentic swarms". Volumetric and L7 blocking stays with the CDN, WAF or bot manager. |

### 8.2 Election infrastructure (voter info, registration portals, results)
**Attack shapes seen in public reports (high level)**
- **Bot-driven floods against a portal:** Georgia SoS absentee-ballot portal, Oct 14, 2024. About 420,000 requests from IPs worldwide. Officials added a human-verification check and traffic dropped within about 30 min. The portal stayed up and no data was compromised. [R] AP https://apnews.com/article/georgia-elections-2024-website-attack-absentee-ballots-4a7244637b28d5a1d755246f5c57a7a5 ; CBS https://www.cbsnews.com/news/georgia-secretary-of-state-office-cyberattack/
- **DDoS on public election-info sites on election day:** Mississippi, Nov 8, 2022. Intermittently unreachable. Voting and counting were unaffected. A pro-Russian group claimed it. [P] https://www.sos.ms.gov/news/election-day-update-0 ; [R] https://therecord.media/mississippi-election-websites-knocked-out-by-ddos-attack
- **Voter registration databases:** CISA's 2026 plan says attempts happened in all 50 states and succeeded in at least 20 (§2, evidence not published).
- **Impersonation and social engineering of officials:** AI phishing and deepfakes (FBI/CISA PSA, Oct 16, 2026, §2). Lookalike domains and fake results pages are a known pattern; Sentinel's CT/dnstwist watch covers that.
- **Agentic version of the same shapes** (inference, not an observed 2026 election case): cheap, adaptive, human-paced automation that spreads across many residential IPs. It goes after lookup and registration forms, ballot-request or cancellation flows, and results pages at peak. It is low-and-slow enough to stay under per-IP rate limits. **I found no primary-source report of an agentic-swarm attack on a U.S. election site in 2026.** Treat this as a design threat, not a documented incident.

**What free help already exists:** election offices can get **free enterprise CDN/WAF/DDoS** from Cloudflare's Athenian Project (state, county and municipal election sites) [P] https://www.cloudflare.com/athenian/ and Google **Project Shield** (election sites; CISA lists it) [P] https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/services/project-shield . CISA offers free canaries, scanning, web-app scanning and RVAs (§2). **So Sentinel must not be sold as the blocking layer. It sits on top of those, working as the operator's console.**

**What "protect" can mean, legally and politically**
- Elections are run by states and localities. CISA's role is voluntary, no-cost support (the 2026 plan frames everything as offered services). A tool can only promise **availability, integrity and accurate presentation of the office's own published information.** That means the page is up, it is the page the office published, the scripts are the ones the office approved, and changes to the registration database are logged and reversible.
- It must **not** decide what is true, moderate speech, label content or touch voter eligibility. It also must **not** run on or connect to **certified voting systems** (tabulators, EMS). Those are governed by EAC VVSG certification and state law (background knowledge; not re-opened). Sentinel is out of scope there.
- Any wording like "secures the election" is an overclaim and a reputational and political liability.

### 8.3 Banking / financial services
**What the primary sources ask for**
- **PCI DSS v4.0.1 Req. 6.4.3 and 11.6.1**, mandatory since **Mar 31, 2025**. 6.4.3: every payment-page script is authorized, integrity-assured and inventoried with a justification. 11.6.1: change and tamper detection on **security-impacting HTTP headers and script contents as received by the consumer browser**, at least weekly or at a risk-based frequency. [P] PCI SSC guidance (Mar 2025) https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/coffee-with-the-council-podcast-guidance-for-pci-dss-e-commerce-requirements-effective-after-31-march-2025 (the guidance PDF is mirrored by acquirers, e.g., https://developer.swedbankpay.com/assets/documents/guidance-for-pci-dss-points.pdf). **This is almost exactly Sentinel's v0.1 third-party-script inventory and served-script probe,** and the Polymarket failure mode is the classic e-skimming pattern applied to a trading session.
- **FFIEC "Authentication and Access to Financial Institution Services and Systems"** (Aug 11, 2021): risk-based, layered security, MFA where single-factor is inadequate, monitoring, logging and reporting across customers, employees, third parties, service accounts and APIs. [P] https://www.ffiec.gov/news/press-releases/2021/pr-08-11
- **FFIEC CAT was sunset on Aug 31, 2025.** FFIEC points institutions to **NIST CSF 2.0, CISA CPGs (including financial-sector CPGs), the CRI Cyber Profile and CIS Controls**, and endorses no tool. [P] https://www.ffiec.gov/resources/cat ; OCC Bulletin 2024-25 https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2024/bulletin-2024-25.html
- **Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships** (OCC/FDIC/Fed, Jun 6, 2023): banks remain responsible when using third parties. It covers due diligence, contracts, ongoing monitoring and termination. [P] https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management . This applies both ways: Sentinel helps *monitor* third parties (scripts, vendors), and Sentinel *is* a third party the bank must diligence.
- **Computer-Security Incident Notification rule** (86 FR 66424, Nov 23, 2021; compliance May 1, 2022). Banks notify their regulator **within 36 hours** of determining a notification incident. **Bank service providers** must notify affected banks as soon as possible for incidents disrupting service for **≥4 hours**. [P] https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/11/23/2021-25510/computer-security-incident-notification-requirements-for-banking-organizations-and-their-bank . So a **Managed Sentinel** sold to banks likely carries service-provider notification duties (needs counsel). Freeze Desk timers should include the 36-hour regulatory clock.
- **FinCEN FIN-2024-Alert004** (Nov 13, 2024): GenAI deepfakes used to get past identity verification, authentication and CDD. Red flags include declined MFA, suspicious live-verification behavior and device or geo inconsistencies. SAR keyword "FIN-2024-DEEPFAKEFRAUD". [P] https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-issues-alert-fraud-schemes-involving-deepfake-media-targeting-financial
- **NYDFS** (23 NYCRR 500). Oct 16, 2024 AI letter [P] https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters/il20241016-cyber-risks-ai-and-strategies-combat-related-risks . **May 21, 2026 "Heightened Cybersecurity Risks Associated with Frontier AI Models"**: no new requirements, but it urges faster vulnerability remediation, **dependency maps** and coordination with critical third parties, **monitoring and validating third-party code**, human oversight of AI-generated code, heightened monitoring and alerting, and testing resilience procedures more often. [P] https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters/20260521-heightened-cybersecurity-risks-assoc-with-frontier-ai-models
- **Open banking (CFPB §1033):** the rule is codified at 12 CFR 1033 but **enjoined** (E.D. Ky., Oct 29, 2025). Compliance dates are stayed and a reconsideration NPRM has been at OIRA since Aug 2026. [P] https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/other-applicable-requirements/personal-financial-data-rights/ ; [R] https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/08/06/cfpb-sends-new-section-1033-open-banking-proposal-to-oira-for-review/ . Data-access APIs still carry aggregator and agent traffic. Sentinel's lanes (verified-agent vs. unknown automation) and per-key behavioral budgets help there, but **the rule's interface requirements are in flux, so don't market "1033 compliance".**

**Fit next to a bank SOC**
- Banks already run a SIEM/SOAR, fraud platforms (device intelligence, behavioral biometrics, transaction monitoring), a bot manager and a CDN/WAF, all under model-risk and change-management governance. **Sentinel does not replace any of them.** It fits as: (a) a **PCI 11.6.1 / 6.4.3 script-integrity and evidence** module; (b) a **Freeze Desk** that times and documents containment and regulatory clocks; (c) a **change-card front end** into the bank's existing change management; (d) **Sigma/OCSF feeds into the SIEM.** It should not do fraud decisions, transaction blocking, SAR filing or customer authentication.
- **Agentic fraud/ATO** (credential stuffing by agents, synthetic-identity account opening, mule networks) is a fraud-platform problem. Sentinel adds cross-surface correlation (lanes and swarm score across login, signup and API) and pages humans. It **cannot see transaction-level fraud** unless the bank feeds it, and it should not.

### 9.2 Red-team signal (what defenders are up against; not components)
- **Stealth browsers for agents are far ahead of OSS bot detection on stars:** CloakHQ/CloakBrowser (~32.6k ★, MIT), jo-inc/camofox-browser (~12.1k ★, pitched as "stealth headless browser for AI agents"), SeleniumBase CDP mode (~13.2k), rebrowser-patches (~1.5k), invisible_playwright (~2.5k). Plus TLS-impersonating HTTP clients: curl_cffi (~6.8k), surf (~1.8k), httpcloak (~1.4k), wreq (~1.1k). By contrast BotD has about 1.5k ★.
- **Implication:** client-side fingerprinting and UA/JA3 checks alone are losing. Sentinel's bet has to be **behavioral, cross-surface correlation** (swarm score, lane budgets), **cryptographic identity for good agents** (Web Bot Auth), and **cost** (Anubis PoW), not signature matching. This matches the Imperva/Thales data in §1.4.
- Repos like `niespodd/browser-fingerprinting` (~5.2k, "how to defeat anti-bot") show which detection signals are already public, so assume attackers know them.
- One search hit (`ikow/php2shell-poc`, ~1.9k ★) is an exploit PoC repo bundling "detection rules". **Excluded:** don't ingest rules from PoC repos without separate review.

### 9.3 Surprises
1. **Signature-based SRI is being standardized** (`draft-west-sri-signature-profile`, Mike West's repo active today). It is the cleanest long-term fix for the Oct 29 vendor-script failure.
2. **There's almost no serious OSS for PCI 6.4.3/11.6.1** (but a mature *commercial* market exists; see §10.3). Search found only toy or agent-built repos (1 star or fewer). That's a real gap, so Sentinel's v0.1 script-integrity module could become *the* open implementation, and it's a strong bank wedge.
3. **Copyleft drift in "safe" projects:** Unleash is AGPL-3.0, Flipt is fair-source FCL, OpenZeppelin Monitor is AGPL, and JA4+ uses a restrictive FoxIO license.
4. **Archived or stale incident tooling:** Netflix Dispatch archived (Sep 2025), monzo/response stale (2024), and CISA's LME archived (May 2026). Incident-as-code is wide open, and the Freeze Desk has little OSS competition.
5. **WAF-as-code across vendors is barely solved:** octorules (4 ★, Feb 2026) is the only multi-provider attempt found. Sentinel's change cards could adopt its plan/apply model.
6. **Election security OSS on GitHub is mostly blockchain-voting toys.** Serious civic projects are voting and audit systems (VotingWorks, ElectionGuard) that are out of Sentinel's scope. There's no open "election office website ops" toolkit, so that's open space for the civic profile.
7. **go-away and iocaine are still not on GitHub.** Only a Traefik iocaine middleware (1 ★) exists there, so their licenses remain unverified.

**How this changes Sentinel's design**
1. **The swarm score must be multi-layer and behavior-first.** It combines TLS/H2 (JA4 core), header-consistency checks, request timing, and (optionally, owner-deployed) in-page interaction telemetry. Browser fingerprint alone is a weak feature. This replaces v1's vaguer "cross-route/IP/account features".
2. **Early-request classification works,** with network features in the first ~3 requests, so lane decisions can happen before a form is submitted.
3. **CAPTCHAs drop to a last-resort rung** on the friction ladder. Prefer PoW (Anubis), step-up auth, signed-agent lanes and rate budgets. Note that PoW was also bypassed in 2606.30119, so it adds cost, not certainty.
4. **Stealth tooling is itself a signal.** Inconsistency features (UA vs. TLS vs. header order) matter more than "looks headless".
5. **Tripwire confirmation by timing** (Palisade) fits Sentinel's alert-only tripwires. Asking an agent questions and timing its replies stays defensive. Instructing it to run code is still forbidden (§6.4).
6. **Privacy and ethics:** in-page behavioral telemetry is invasive, and 2605.14786 shows it can fingerprint users' agents. Sentinel must make it **opt-in, owner-deployed, aggregated, and short-retention**, and the civic profile **disables it by default.**
7. **Sentinel's own agent:** adopt CaMeL-style **control/data separation**. Ingested content (logs, X posts, page bodies) can never change which tools run, only fill typed fields. That matches the existing proposal-only design.
8. **Measured claims only:** academic detection rates are lab results on a handful of agents. We **must not** cite "97% detection" as a Sentinel capability.

**How this changes Sentinel's design**
1. **Response clocks in minutes.** Breakout is 29 min on average and as fast as 27 s, and exfiltration takes 72 min for the fastest quarter. A human-approval-only Freeze Desk is too slow without **pre-approved envelopes**. The v0.1 targets (page in ≤5 min, freeze within minutes of approval) are the minimum. KPIs should report against these vendor benchmarks.
2. **IP reputation is a weak signal.** Agent swarms run from compromised *legitimate* cloud IPs (GTIG). Lanes and swarm scores must not depend on IP blocklists. CrowdSec stays a supplement only.
3. **"Signed" does not mean "safe."** UNC6780 produced *valid* SLSA L3 attestations with stolen OIDC tokens. Sentinel's self-defense therefore needs **(a)** signer-identity pinning (expected repo, workflow and ref, not just "has a valid attestation"), **(b)** release **two-person approval outside CI**, **(c)** Rekor monitoring for unexpected signatures on Sentinel's identity, and **(d)** the same checks offered to owners as a watcher ("new signed release of a dependency from an unexpected workflow").
4. **Don't trust LLM scanners blindly.** Attackers embed text meant to make LLM security scanners refuse or skip. Any LLM triage step in Sentinel must treat refusals and skips as "**needs human review**", never "clean".
5. **AI coding-assistant config paths** (`.claude/`, `.cursor/`, `.vscode/`, MCP configs) are now persistence and prompt-injection spots (GTIG). Add them to the served-content and repo-drift watch, and add MCP server pinning to the v0.6 AI-endpoint module.
6. **Exploits stay the #1 vector** (Mandiant) and n-day weaponization is speeding up (GTIG), so KEV↔SBOM matching plus virtual-patch proposals stay in v0.1/v0.2.
7. **Voice phishing at #2** and the FBI/CISA deepfake PSA argue for **out-of-band verification steps inside Freeze Desk playbooks.** Never act on a freeze request that arrives over a single channel.
